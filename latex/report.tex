\author{Team SWINQ}
\date{3 October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct the three-dimensional orientation of a tennis racket through one forehand from the
6-axis inertial measurement unit (IMU) inside a SWINQ string dampener: 400 samples at
\SI{416}{Hz}, starting mid-swing, with the accelerometer saturated at \SI{16}{g} just before impact.
Orientation is obtained by strapdown integration of the gyroscope with exact exponential quaternion
steps (verified to $0.03\dg$ on a synthetic $500\dg$/s moving-axis rotation). Two unsynchronised,
uncalibrated videos are turned into a 3D reference by a two-camera bundle adjustment that uses the
racket itself as the calibration object. From the sensor alone we report a peak angular velocity of
$1720\dg$/s, an estimated racket-head speed of \SI{24.6}{m/s} (\SI{89}{km/h}) at impact, a
reconstructed acceleration peak of about \SI{30}{g} above the saturation limit, and a string
vibration aliased to \SI{164.5}{Hz} (most likely \SI{581}{Hz}). The sensor-only orientation agrees
with video to $2$--$11\dg$ in the early swing but drifts in the fast phase. A Gaussian-process (GP)
drift correction on the orientation error reduces the leave-one-out error against video from a mean
of $88.4\dg$ to $12.4\dg$ (median $8.3\dg$). We document every model, the numerical checks, the
hypotheses we ruled out, and the remaining limitations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and data}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Goal}
The challenge asks for a 3D model of a tennis stroke relying on the sensor data, robust to sensor
noise and accelerometer saturation, that shows the racket's orientation and key motion parameters
and can be extended to full 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) tracking. Two videos of the same stroke
are provided for reference. We focus on orientation, the rotational half of 6-DoF, which is fully
observable from a gyroscope up to its initial value.

\subsection{IMU data}
The file \texttt{raw\_data.csv} contains $N=400$ samples with columns
$(a_x,a_y,a_z)$ in $g$ and $(\omega_x,\omega_y,\omega_z)$ in degrees per second. There are no
timestamps; the sample rate is $f_s=\SI{416}{Hz}$, so sample $k$ is at $t_k=k/f_s$ and the
record spans $N/f_s=\SI{0.962}{s}$. Table~\ref{tab:imu} lists the facts that shape the method.

\subsection{Sensor frame}
The sensor sits on the string bed near the throat. Its right-handed axes are: $x$ along the handle
towards the butt cap, $y$ across the string face, and $z$ normal to the string face
(Fig.~\ref{fig:racket}). The $x$ axis is confirmed by the data: in rigid rotation about a pivot at
distance $r$ along $-x$, the accelerometer's $x$ component carries the centripetal term
\begin{equation}
a_x \approx r\left(\omega_y^2+\omega_z^2\right) = r\,\omega_\perp^2 ,
\label{eq:centripetal}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_\perp$ is the rotation rate perpendicular to the handle. Before impact (samples
0--181) the correlation between $a_x$ and $\omega_\perp^2$ is $0.92$; after impact (231--399) it is
$0.99$. The sign convention of positive rotation was not known in advance and is determined from
the video (Section~\ref{sec:sign}).

\subsection{Videos and anchors}
Two 1280$\times$720 videos at about 30 frames/s show the stroke from the front and from behind at an
oblique angle. Impact is at front frame 131 and rear frame 172, and front frame $n$ corresponds to
rear frame $n+41$. For the 29 frames inside the IMU window (front 117--145), four racket points
(butt, throat, tip, edge) were clicked in both views. Each moment $i$ maps to an IMU sample
$s_i = 200 + f_s\,t_i$ with $t_i$ the time from impact. We verified that the decoded presentation
timestamps match $t_i$ exactly in both views, and we found one labelling error: in front frame 118
the four labels are rotated by one position (edge$\to$butt, butt$\to$throat, throat$\to$tip,
tip$\to$edge), which we correct at load time.

\section{Rotation mathematics}\label{sec:quat}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Unit quaternions}
An orientation is a unit quaternion $\q=(q_w,\qv)$, $\qv=(q_x,q_y,q_z)$, $\norm{\q}=1$. With the
Hamilton product
\begin{equation}
\q_1\qmul\q_2 = \bigl(w_1w_2-\qv_1\!\cdot\!\qv_2,\;\; w_1\qv_2+w_2\qv_1+\qv_1\times\qv_2\bigr),
\end{equation}
$\q$ rotates a body-frame vector $\ve{u}$ into the world frame as
$(0,\ve{u}_{\mathrm{w}}) = \q\qmul(0,\ve{u}_{\mathrm{b}})\qmul\q^{-1}$, with $\q^{-1}=(q_w,-\qv)$.
The equivalent rotation matrix is
\begin{equation}
\R(\q)=\begin{pmatrix}
1-2(q_y^2+q_z^2) & 2(q_xq_y-q_wq_z) & 2(q_xq_z+q_wq_y)\\
2(q_xq_y+q_wq_z) & 1-2(q_x^2+q_z^2) & 2(q_yq_z-q_wq_x)\\
2(q_xq_z-q_wq_y) & 2(q_yq_z+q_wq_x) & 1-2(q_x^2+q_y^2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Since $\q$ and $-\q$ are the same rotation, we keep integrated series sign-continuous
($\q_k\cdot\q_{k-1}>0$), which matters for interpolation and for animation in Blender.

\subsection{Exponential and logarithm maps}
A rotation by angle $\theta$ about unit axis $\hat{\ve{n}}$ is the rotation vector
$\ve{\phi}=\theta\hat{\ve{n}}$. The exponential map is
\begin{equation}
\Exp(\ve{\phi}) = \Bigl(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2},\; \frac{\sin(\theta/2)}{\theta}\,\ve{\phi}\Bigr),
\qquad \theta=\norm{\ve{\phi}},
\label{eq:exp}
\end{equation}
using the series $\sin(\theta/2)/\theta\approx \tfrac12-\theta^2/48$ for $\theta<10^{-8}$. Its
inverse, for $q_w\ge 0$, is $\Log(\q)=\dfrac{2\operatorname{atan2}(\norm{\qv},q_w)}{\norm{\qv}}\,\qv$,
which returns angles in $[0,\pi]$.

\subsection{Distance and interpolation}
The geodesic angle between two orientations is
\begin{equation}
d(\q_1,\q_2)=2\arccos\bigl|\q_1\cdot\q_2\bigr| ,
\end{equation}
and spherical interpolation at fraction $u\in[0,1]$ is
$\operatorname{slerp}(\q_1,\q_2,u)=\q_1\qmul\Exp\bigl(u\,\Log(\q_1^{-1}\qmul\q_2)\bigr)$.
We use it to evaluate an orientation at fractional sample indices, e.g.\ $s_i=19.7$.

% =====================================================================
\section{Gyroscope strapdown integration}\label{sec:gyro}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Model}
The gyroscope measures body-frame angular velocity. With an unknown constant bias $\ve{b}$ and a
rotation-sign convention $\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}$, the rate used for integration is
\begin{equation}
\w_k = \sigma\,\frac{\pi}{180}\bigl(\tilde{\w}_k-\ve{b}\bigr)\quad[\mathrm{rad/s}].
\end{equation}
Orientation obeys the kinematic equation $\dot{\q}=\tfrac12\,\q\qmul(0,\w)$. Over one sample
interval $\Delta t=1/f_s$ we take the mean of the two end rates and apply the exact exponential:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\q_{k+1}=\q_k\qmul\Exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\w_k+\w_{k+1}\bigr)\Delta t\Bigr),\qquad
\q_0 \text{ given.}\;}
\label{eq:integrate}
\end{equation}
Right-multiplication applies the increment in the current body frame. In words: the new angle is
the old angle plus the small turn made during the last $2.4$\,ms.

\subsection{Accuracy}
Equation~\eqref{eq:integrate} is exact whenever the rotation axis is fixed during the interval. When
the axis moves, the commutator $\tfrac12\w\times\dot{\w}$ (the coning term) is neglected, giving a
local error of order $\Delta t^3$ and a global error of order $\Delta t^2$. We verified this on the
analytic rotation $\R(t)=\R_z(\alpha t)\,\R_y(\beta t)$ with $\alpha=6$, $\beta=9$\,rad/s, whose
body rate is
\begin{equation}
\w_{\mathrm b}(t)=\R_y(\beta t)^{\!\top}(0,0,\alpha)^{\top}+(0,\beta,0)^{\top},
\end{equation}
a moving axis at roughly $500\dg$/s. Table~\ref{tab:conv} shows the maximum orientation error over
one second: it falls by a factor of 4 per doubling of the rate, confirming second-order convergence.

\begin{table}[H]\centering\small
\caption{Integration error on an analytic moving-axis rotation (1\,s, about $500\dg$/s).}\label{tab:conv}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}\toprule
Sample rate (Hz) & 208 & 416 & 832 & 1664 \\\midrule
Max error (deg) & 0.1073 & \textbf{0.0268} & 0.0067 & 0.0017 \\

On the real data, integrating the gyro at 416\,Hz and after $4\times$ cubic-spline upsampling
differ by at most $0.18\dg$ over the swing ($0.04\dg$ at the end), so step size is not a limitation.
Unit tests also cover: zero rate (orientation constant), a constant single-axis rate (exact to
$10^{-9}\dg$), bias cancelling a constant rate, sign reversal, composition order against a
reference product of scipy rotations, and continuity through $720\dg$.

\subsection{Sensitivity to bias and sign}
A constant bias $\ve{b}$ adds a spurious rotation that grows with time. On this record a bias of
$1\dg$/s on any single axis moves the final orientation by $0.3$--$0.6\dg$. The sign matters far
more: integrated with $\sigma=+1$ the racket turns $163\dg$ from start to impact, with
$\sigma=-1$ it turns $134\dg$, and the two histories differ by up to $180\dg$. The total rotation
path $\sum_k d(\q_k,\q_{k+1})$ is $571\dg$ for either sign.

\section{Cameras and racket from the videos}\label{sec:ba}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Camera model}
Each camera $c\in\{1,2\}$ is a pinhole with square pixels, the principal point at the image centre
$\ve{c}_0=(640,360)$, no distortion and unknown focal length $f_c$. Camera 1 (front) defines the
world frame. A racket pose $(\R_i,\ve{t}_i)$ at moment $i$ maps a model point $\ve{P}_j$ (sensor
frame) into the two cameras:
\begin{align}
\ve{X}^{(1)}_{ij} &= \R_i\ve{P}_j+\ve{t}_i, &
\ve{X}^{(2)}_{ij} &= \R_{21}\ve{X}^{(1)}_{ij}+\ve{t}_{21},\\
\pi_c(\ve{X}) &= f_c\begin{pmatrix}X/Z\\ Y/Z\end{pmatrix}+\ve{c}_0 . &&
\end{align}
The racket model is planar ($Z=0$ for all four points) with butt, throat and tip on the $x$ axis.
Its scale is fixed by the butt--tip length $L=\SI{0.685}{m}$, which makes the reconstruction metric.

\subsection{Bundle adjustment}
The unknowns are $\log f_1,\log f_2$, the camera-2 pose $(\ve{\phi}_{21},\ve{t}_{21})$, optionally
three geometry parameters, and one 6-DoF pose per moment: $8+3+6\times 29=185$ parameters against
$2\times29\times4\times2=464$ pixel residuals. We minimise
\begin{equation}
E=\sum_{c,i,j}\rho\!\left(\bigl\|\pi_c(\ve{X}^{(c)}_{ij})-\ve{u}^{(c)}_{ij}\bigr\|^2\right),\qquad
\rho(z)=2\delta^2\Bigl(\sqrt{1+z/\delta^2}-1\Bigr),
\label{eq:ba}
\end{equation}
the soft-$L_1$ robust loss with knee $\delta=\SI{3}{px}$. It behaves like least squares for small
residuals and like an absolute value for outliers such as motion-blurred clicks.

\paragraph{Conditioning: weak perspective.} The racket is about \SI{7}{m} from camera 1 and spans
only about \SI{150}{px}, so its image scale fixes the ratio $f_1/Z$ well but $f_1$ and $Z$
separately poorly. We therefore parametrise each translation as
\begin{equation}
\ve{t}_i = Z_i\,(a_i,\,b_i,\,1)^{\top},\qquad Z_i = f_1\,e^{-s_i},
\end{equation}
where $(a_i,b_i)$ is the ray to the throat and $s_i=\log(f_1/Z_i)$ the log image scale. The poorly
determined direction ``scale $f_1$ and every depth together'' then becomes a single coordinate,
and the trust-region solver converges in tens instead of thousands of iterations. The Jacobian is
small (about $464\times185$), so we compute it densely by grouped finite differences (each pose
touches only its own 16 residuals) and use the exact trust-region step.

\subsection{Initialisation without a homography}
Standard planar PnP (IPPE) estimates a homography, which needs four points with no three collinear;
here butt, throat and tip are collinear, so it fails. Instead, for every view we run a batched
Levenberg--Marquardt solve of the 6-DoF pose from the 24 rotations of the octahedral group, keep
every distinct solution with RMS error below \SI{15}{px} and positive depth, and obtain the expected
two mirror-ambiguous poses per view. For each moment, every pairing of a camera-1 and a camera-2
candidate implies a relative rotation $\R_{21}=\R^{(2)}\R^{(1)\top}$. The rotation agreed on by the
most moments (within $15\dg$) seeds $\R_{21}$, $\ve{t}_{21}$ and the per-moment poses.

\subsection{Mirror ambiguity repair}
A planar object seen nearly edge-on has two poses that reproject almost equally well. After each
joint solve we hold the cameras fixed and, for every moment, re-fit its pose from all single-view
candidates of both cameras (camera-2 candidates mapped by $\R^{(1)}=\R_{21}^{\top}\R^{(2)}$,
$\ve{t}^{(1)}=\R_{21}^{\top}(\ve{t}^{(2)}-\ve{t}_{21})$), keeping the lowest robust cost. Moments
0 and 10 were repaired this way, and afterwards both focal-length starts ($f=1000$ and $2400$\,px)
converge to the same solution, with orientations agreeing to $0.02\dg$.

\subsection{Racket geometry}
The nominal geometry (butt $+0.36$\,m, tip $-0.33$\,m from the throat) does not fit the clicks: in
both views the clicked throat lies at $0.33$ of the way from butt to tip, not $0.52$. We therefore
free the throat fraction $\lambda$ and the edge point $(e_x,e_y)$, with the length fixed:
\begin{equation}
\ve{P}_{\text{butt}}=(\lambda L,0,0),\quad \ve{P}_{\text{tip}}=(-(1-\lambda)L,0,0),\quad
\ve{P}_{\text{edge}}=(e_x,e_y,0).
\end{equation}
The refined values are butt $+0.231$\,m, tip $-0.454$\,m and edge $(-0.272,\,+0.149)$\,m. Refining
lowers the robust cost from $2908$ to $2430$.

\subsection{Uncertainty per moment}
For each moment we linearise its 16 residuals with respect to a local body-frame rotation
$\R_i\Exp([\ve{\delta}]_\times)$ and its translation, holding the cameras fixed, and set the pixel
noise to the robust estimate $\hat\sigma=1.4826\,\operatorname{median}|r|=\SI{3.24}{px}$:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_i=\hat\sigma^2\,(\mathbf{J}_i^{\top}\mathbf{J}_i)^{-1},\qquad
\sigma_{\text{axis}}=\sqrt{[\Sigma_i]_{kk}}\;(k=1,2,3).
\end{equation}
Local perturbations avoid the singularity of the rotation-vector parametrisation near $180\dg$.

\section{Joint IMU--video fit and diagnostics}\label{sec:joint}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Model}
The planned approach fits the integrated gyro to the 29 video orientations $\q^{\mathrm v}_i$:
\begin{equation}
\q^{\mathrm v}_i \;\approx\; \q_0\qmul \mathbf{Q}\!\left(s_i+\tau;\,\ve{b},\sigma\right)\qmul\ve{m},
\end{equation}
with $\q_0$ the start orientation, $\mathbf{Q}$ the gyro rotation from sample 0
(Eq.~\ref{eq:integrate} with $\q_0=\mathbf{1}$), $\tau$ a time offset bounded to $\pm\SI{50}{ms}$,
and $\ve{m}$ a small sensor-to-racket mounting rotation. The residual is the error rotation vector
in the racket frame,
\begin{equation}
\ve{r}_i=\Log\!\Bigl(\bigl(\q_0\qmul\mathbf{Q}(s_i+\tau)\qmul\ve{m}\bigr)^{-1}\qmul\q^{\mathrm v}_i\Bigr),
\qquad \norm{\ve{r}_i}=d\bigl(\text{IMU},\text{video}\bigr),
\end{equation}
minimised with a soft-$L_1$ loss (knee $5\dg$) and weight $0.2$ on moments in samples 195--230. The
gyro is padded by holding its end rates for 30 samples, so that $\tau$ can move the end moments
past the data without the cost becoming discontinuous. $\q_0$ is initialised in closed form
(Section~\ref{sec:q0}).

\subsection{Outcome}
The fit has no physical solution (Table~\ref{tab:joint}). Both signs need biases of hundreds of
degrees per second and a mounting rotation of $58$--$150\dg$, and the time offset runs into its
bound.

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\paragraph{Rotation magnitudes.} Comparing the angle turned between consecutive moments (which does
not depend on $\q_0$) shows that through the fast phase the gyro measures 2--3 times more rotation
than the video: for example, between moments 6 and 9 the gyro turns $55\dg$ and the video $18\dg$.
The accelerometer supports the gyro scale: Eq.~\eqref{eq:centripetal} gives a physically sensible
pivot radius of \SI{0.65}{m}. If the gyro read $k$ times too high, the true rate would be
$\omega/k$ and the true radius $k^2\times\SI{0.65}{m}$, i.e.\ 4--\SI{6}{m} for $k=2.5$--$3$,
which is not physical for a forehand.

\paragraph{Axis conventions.} We tested all 48 signed permutation matrices $\mathbf{P}$
($\tilde{\w}\mapsto\mathbf{P}\tilde{\w}$, both handednesses). The median error between consecutive
moments is $13.3\dg$ for the best permutation, against $17.8\dg$ for the stated axes with
$\sigma=-1$. No mapping removes the discrepancy.

\paragraph{Sample rate.} Rescaling time by $f_s'\in[416,1666]$\,Hz (equivalently, integrating
$\tilde{\w}\,f_s/f_s'$ and mapping $s_i=200+t_if_s'$) never yields a physical fit
(Table~\ref{tab:rate}).

\paragraph{Pixel-space fit.} To rule out errors in the per-frame video orientations (for instance
mirror flips), we also fitted the IMU model directly to the clicked pixels, with the cameras fixed
and a free translation per moment. The best results are a median of \SI{10.4}{px} (sign $-1$) and
\SI{11.1}{px} (sign $+1$), again with biases of $90$--$450\dg$/s and mountings of $70$--$140\dg$.
We conclude that, under the stated facts, the IMU and the videos cannot be reconciled by a
constant-parameter model. The most likely causes are the sensor-to-video time alignment and the
30\,fps, heavily blurred video in the fast phase.

\section{Sensor-only orientation}\label{sec:sensoronly}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Start orientation by quaternion averaging}\label{sec:q0}
A gyroscope observes only changes in orientation, and with no still period gravity cannot fix the
start. We take the start orientation from the first $n=5$ video moments, assuming zero bias and no
mounting correction. Each moment gives one estimate
$\hat{\q}_{0,i}=\q^{\mathrm v}_i\qmul\mathbf{Q}(s_i)^{-1}$. Their chordal L2 mean is
\begin{equation}
\q_0=\arg\max_{\norm{\q}=1}\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(\q\cdot\hat{\q}_{0,i}\bigr)^2
=\text{eigenvector of }\mathbf{M}=\sum_i\hat{\q}_{0,i}\hat{\q}_{0,i}^{\top}
\text{ with the largest eigenvalue,}
\end{equation}
which is invariant to the $\pm\q$ sign of each estimate.

\subsection{Rotation sign}\label{sec:sign}
With the start fixed this way, sign $\sigma=-1$ matches the five anchor moments to
$2.7,\,2.0,\,2.8,\,2.7,\,4.4\dg$, while $\sigma=+1$ gives $17.2,\,9.1,\,2.2,\,7.8,\,19.2\dg$. We
adopt $\sigma=-1$. The sensor-only orientation is then Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrate} with $\ve b=\ve 0$,
$\sigma=-1$ and this $\q_0$. It agrees with video to within $11\dg$ up to moment 6 (sample 89,
0.27\,s before impact) and then drifts to $100$--$170\dg$.

\section{Motion parameters from the sensor}\label{sec:params}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Pivot radius}
Fitting Eq.~\eqref{eq:centripetal} through the origin on the unclipped pre-impact samples
$\mathcal{K}=\{0,\dots,181\}$ gives the closed-form least-squares radius
\begin{equation}
\hat r=\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal K} a_{x,k}\,\omega_{\perp,k}^2}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\omega_{\perp,k}^4}
=\SI{0.65}{m},\qquad \operatorname{corr}(a_x,\omega_\perp^2)=0.92 ,
\end{equation}
with $a_x$ in m/s$^2$ and $\omega_\perp$ in rad/s. This is the distance from the effective pivot
(wrist and elbow) to the sensor.

\subsection{Speeds}
For rotation about the pivot, a point at distance $\rho$ from it moves at $v=\omega_\perp\rho$.
Just before impact (sample 199), $\omega_\perp=1527.5\dg/\mathrm{s}=26.66$\,rad/s, so
\begin{align}
v_{\text{sensor}} &= 26.66\times0.65 = \SI{17.4}{m/s},\\
v_{\text{head}} &= 26.66\times(0.65+0.272) = \SI{24.6}{m/s}\approx\SI{89}{km/h},\\
v_{\text{tip}} &= 26.66\times(0.65+0.454) = \SI{29.5}{m/s}.
\end{align}
These are estimates: they assume rotation about a single fixed pivot and ignore its translation.

\subsection{Saturation repair}
On samples 182--199, $a_x$ is pinned at \SI{15.96}{g}. Over the whole forward swing, measured $a_x$
rises roughly linearly while $\hat r\,\omega_\perp^2$ rises quadratically, because the pivot radius
shrinks as the wrist releases and gravity and tangential terms add an offset. We therefore fit a
local affine model on the last 12 unclipped samples $\mathcal K'=\{170,\dots,181\}$,
\begin{equation}
a_x \approx r'\,\omega_\perp^2 + c,\qquad (r',c)=\arg\min\sum_{k\in\mathcal K'}
\bigl(a_{x,k}-r'\omega_{\perp,k}^2-c\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
which gives $r'=\SI{0.31}{m}$ and $c=\SI{6.78}{g}$ with correlation $1.00$, joins the measured curve
continuously, and fills the gap with a peak of \SI{29.5}{g}. The global radius $\hat r$ would give
\SI{47}{g}, so the true peak lies roughly in 30--\SI{47}{g}; we quote the conservative value.

\subsection{String vibration and aliasing}
After impact, $a_z$ rings. On 128 post-impact samples, detrended with a quadratic and Hann-windowed,
the zero-padded spectrum peaks at $f_{\text{obs}}=\SI{164.5}{Hz}$. A sampled sinusoid at $f$ is
indistinguishable from one at $|f-kf_s|$, so the possible true frequencies are
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{true}}\in\{\,f_{\text{obs}},\; f_s-f_{\text{obs}},\; f_s+f_{\text{obs}},\dots\}
=\{164.5,\;251.5,\;580.5,\dots\}\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{equation}
String-bed modes typically lie at 500--650\,Hz, so \SI{581}{Hz} is the most likely value.

\section{Gaussian-process drift correction}\label{sec:gp}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Error signal}
Let $\q^{\mathrm s}(s)$ be the sensor-only orientation. At each moment the error rotation vector,
in the racket frame, is
\begin{equation}
\ve{e}_i=\Log\!\bigl(\q^{\mathrm s}(s_i)^{-1}\qmul\q^{\mathrm v}_i\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^3 .
\end{equation}
Near $\norm{\ve e}=\pi$ the logarithm flips between $\ve e$ and the equivalent vector
$\ve e-2\pi\,\ve e/\norm{\ve e}$, which would show up as a jump. We unwrap the series by choosing,
for each $i$, whichever of the two forms is closer to $\ve e_{i-1}$, so that $\ve e_i$ is
continuous in time (here $\norm{\ve e_i}$ reaches $264\dg$).

\subsection{GP regression}
Each component $e^{(k)}(t)$, $k=1,2,3$, gets an independent zero-mean GP prior (after
normalisation) with covariance
\begin{equation}
\kappa(t,t')=\sigma_f^2\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(t-t')^2}{2\ell^2}\Bigr)+\sigma_n^2\,\delta_{tt'},
\end{equation}
plus a known per-moment noise variance $\alpha_i$: $(0.02\,\mathrm{rad})^2$ normally, and
$(0.15\,\mathrm{rad})^2$ for moments in samples 195--230 (the impact transient). With
$\mathbf K_{ij}=\kappa(t_i,t_j)+\alpha_i\delta_{ij}$, the posterior at time $t_*$ is
\begin{align}
\mu_*(t_*) &= \ve{k}_*^{\top}\mathbf{K}^{-1}\ve{y}, &
\sigma_*^2(t_*) &= \kappa(t_*,t_*)-\ve{k}_*^{\top}\mathbf{K}^{-1}\ve{k}_*,
\end{align}
with $\ve y=(e^{(k)}_1,\dots,e^{(k)}_{29})$ and $(\ve k_*)_i=\kappa(t_*,t_i)$. The hyperparameters
$(\sigma_f,\ell,\sigma_n)$ maximise the log marginal likelihood
\begin{equation}
\log p(\ve y\mid t)=-\tfrac12\ve y^{\top}\mathbf K^{-1}\ve y-\tfrac12\log\det\mathbf K-\tfrac{29}{2}\log 2\pi ,
\end{equation}
with $\ell\in[0.02,2]$\,s and 3 random restarts. The fitted kernels are
\begin{center}\small
$0.978^2\,\mathrm{RBF}(\ell=0.132)+\mathrm{W}(7.9\!\times\!10^{-4})$,\quad
$0.807^2\,\mathrm{RBF}(\ell=0.111)+\mathrm{W}(6.7\!\times\!10^{-3})$,\quad
$1.30^2\,\mathrm{RBF}(\ell=0.137)+\mathrm{W}(6.4\!\times\!10^{-2})$,
\end{center}
i.e.\ length scales of 0.11--0.14\,s, about four video frames.

\subsection{Correction and uncertainty}
The predicted error is removed at every IMU sample:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\q^{\mathrm c}(t)=\q^{\mathrm s}(t)\qmul\Exp\bigl(\ve\mu_*(t)\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}
and the reported uncertainty per sample is
$\sigma_{\mathrm{GP}}(t)=\sqrt{\sum_k\sigma_*^{(k)}(t)^2}$, with a median of $16.9\dg$ and a
maximum of $22.0\dg$.

\subsection{Leave-one-out validation}
Fitting and scoring on the same 29 moments would be optimistic. For each moment $i$ we fit the GP on
the other 28, predict $\ve\mu_*^{(-i)}(t_i)$, and score
$\varepsilon_i=d\bigl(\q^{\mathrm s}(s_i)\qmul\Exp(\ve\mu_*^{(-i)}(t_i)),\,\q^{\mathrm v}_i\bigr)$.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Sensor versus video.} The central open issue is that the gyro integrates 2--3 times
more rotation than the video shows in the fast phase. The accelerometer independently supports the
gyro scale (Eq.~\ref{eq:centripetal}, $r=\SI{0.65}{m}$), and we ruled out axis conventions, sample
rate, constant bias, mounting rotation and video mirror flips. The remaining suspects are the time
alignment of the two clocks (impact frames are known only to $\pm1$ frame, \SI{33}{ms}, during which
the racket turns up to $57\dg$) and the 30\,fps video with heavy motion blur.
\item \textbf{Fusion.} The GP-corrected orientation fuses sensor and video. It is validated fairly
(leave-one-out), but it is no longer sensor-only, which is why both results are delivered.
\item \textbf{Roll about the handle} is the least constrained video axis ($\approx5\dg$ at
$1\sigma$), because only the edge point carries it.
\item \textbf{Impact transient.} Samples 200--215 are affected by the impact (the gyro jumps by
$466\dg$/s); the GP gives these moments larger noise.
\item \textbf{Speeds} assume a single fixed pivot; a full 6-DoF solution would replace this with
integrated position.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Towards 6-DoF}
Position follows from the accelerometer once orientation is known:
$\ddot{\ve p}_{\mathrm w}=\R(\q)\,\ve a_{\mathrm b}\,g-\ve g_{\mathrm w}$. This requires the
saturation repair of Section~\ref{sec:params} and a strong prior or anchors (here, the stage-3
translations) to contain double-integration drift. The pieces developed here, accurate orientation,
saturation repair and a pivot model, form the base for that step.

\section{Conclusion}
From a single dampener IMU we reconstruct the racket orientation at 416\,Hz with an integrator
verified to hundredths of a degree, repair accelerometer saturation with a physical model, and
extract the stroke's key parameters: $1720\dg$/s peak rotation, about \SI{89}{km/h} head speed,
$85\dg$ of rotation in the last 100\,ms, and about \SI{30}{g} peak load. Against a 3D reference
built from two uncalibrated videos, the sensor-only orientation is accurate in the early swing.
A GP drift correction, validated leave-one-out, brings the mean error to $12.4\dg$ over the whole
stroke. The remaining gap between sensor and video in the fastest phase is documented, not hidden,
and points to precise time synchronisation as the next step.
